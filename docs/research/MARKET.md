  Directory, Swiss Re Capital Markets via its report, the World Bank, the ADB, OECD, IFSCA, a ministry of finance).
- **secondary**: from trade press or a third party that reports someone else's figure, or a primary page that could
  only be read through a search snippet.
- **not found**: searched, no source found. Not the same as "does not exist".

## Conflicts and gaps

- **Outstanding size:** Artemis $65.6B (end H1 2026) and $65.5B (end Q3 2026) vs Swiss Re $64.8B (30 Jun 2026). Both are
  primary data publishers with different scopes; quote the publisher with the figure. "$40B+" is stale (end-2023 era).
- **H1 2026 issuance:** Artemis $17.98B vs Swiss Re $17.6B; **2025 issuance:** Artemis $25.6B (incl. private and non-cat
  144A) vs $23.9B (Artemis, 144A property cat only) vs Swiss Re $24.7B.
- **Jamaica 2026 dates:** World Bank trade date 18 May 2026; Jamaica's Ministry of Finance says "issued" 26 May 2026 (the
  settlement date). Same deal.
- **Peru payout:** $60m (30%) per Peru's Ministry of Economy and Finance (via search; the page did not load) and Artemis;
  one GlobalCapital headline said $150m. **Mexico 2017 payout:** $150m (100%) per the Mexican Finance Ministry as
  reported by Artemis and Mexican press; the ministry's own release was not found. Both payouts stay secondary and are
  left out of the pitch summary.
- **Sponsor-type shares:** only partial, from three different publishers (AM Best, ICMIF, Barclays) with different
  bases; no single current table found.
- **Investor shares:** only a dated (~2017) Artemis article; no current Swiss Re or Aon breakdown found.
- **Issuance costs:** no source gives a cost breakdown or a minimum viable size beyond the Singapore grant rule and the
  qualitative OECD statements.
- **India:** no government, NDMA or GIC Re cat bond or earthquake parametric plan found in a primary source; one
  low-reliability site mentions early talks on a flood/heatwave scheme (not used).
- **Not fully read:** the Swiss Re ILS report itself (figures via Artemis), the Peak Re press release (HTTP 403), the
  AM Best report (figures via Artemis).
- **Dead or blocked links:** [21] (World Bank, 404) and [12] (ADB, 403 to scripts) were read from Wayback Machine
  snapshots of the same pages; the facts used are quoted from those snapshots.
- **India wording:** Black Kite Re 2025-1 (Peak Re) covers Indian earthquake risk, so India is not absent from the cat
  bond market; what is absent is a sovereign cat bond or sovereign parametric cover (OECD, 2024).

## Pitch summary (confirmed facts only)

1. The catastrophe bond market stood at **$65.5 billion outstanding at the end of Q3 2026**, after a record **$25.6 billion
   of issuance in 2025** (Artemis).
2. Sovereign **parametric earthquake** cover is an established product: the World Bank placed **$1.36 billion** for Chile,
   Colombia, Mexico and Peru in 2018 and **$630 million** (bond + swaps) for Chile in 2023, and the ADB issued its first
   parametric earthquake cat bonds (**$160 million**, Kyrgyz Republic and Tajikistan) in 2026.
3. In November 2025 the World Bank confirmed that Hurricane Melissa **triggered a full $150 million parametric payout** for
   Jamaica; in May 2026 Jamaica replaced the cover with a **$200 million** World Bank parametric bond.
4. India has **no sovereign cat bond or sovereign parametric cover** (OECD, 2024), while IFSCA's 2025 working group calls
   India "highly prone to … earthquakes" and recommends building an ILS / cat bond framework in GIFT IFSC.
5. Small and private deals face real costs: a **cat bond lite** exists to skip cost-intensive steps (OECD, 2024). For the
   blockchain-based ILS platform used in the Danish Red Cross volcano bond, the OECD puts the saving at about
   **USD 200,000–400,000 per issue** against traditional settlement; that figure is for that platform, not for
   blockchain in general.
